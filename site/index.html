KL University capstone · DSCI-41 · Visual Intelligence Systems

Roadside video in, traffic counts and forecasts out

A computer-vision pipeline that detects, tracks and counts vehicles from fixed roadside cameras, flags results it should not be trusted on (night, rain, dense occlusion), and forecasts the next few minutes of traffic.

How it works

Each stage is a separate, tested module. Green stages run today; amber ones are the next phases of the project.

  1. Ingest & validateMP4/AVI checks, checksums, corrupt-file rejection
  2. DetectYOLO11n, car · bus · truck · motorcycle · bicycle
  3. TrackByteTrack identities and trajectories
  4. CountVirtual lines, direction, one count per vehicle
  5. Condition checkNight, blur, low contrast, dense occlusion → degraded
  6. PerspectiveHomography, ground-plane counting
  7. Robustness (O3)Occlusion, rain and low-light improvements
  8. Congestion modelLabelled 5-minute-ahead congestion

What the expressway clip revealed

Two counting lines, same total, different vehicles

Motorcycles filter between queued cars and stay hidden until they are already past the upper line, so the tracker never sees them cross it. The totals barely differ, yet one line misses every motorcycle and the other misses every bus and truck. This is why the project reports results per vehicle class, never only in aggregate.

Morning peak versus night

The same camera, the same 23-minute length. At night every frame is flagged, so the counts are labelled degraded instead of being reported as normal.

Project status

    What is not established yet

    • Accuracy. No counting accuracy is claimed. A blind hand-counting protocol, a fixed 17-clip plan and a counting tool are ready; two people must count each clip.
    • Congestion levels. The labelled congestion data requires sign-in to the challenge platform; until it is loaded, only traffic volume is forecast.
    • Robustness gains (O3). Night, rain and occlusion improvements are the next phase and will be judged only by measured gains on the same data and hardware.
    • Condition thresholds. The night, blur and occlusion flags use provisional thresholds that still need calibration.
